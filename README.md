## Business First. Architecture Next. Technology Last.

### Overview

PPD-GVAR is an enterprise architecture case-study portfolio developed as part of the **Global Learning Solution Platform (GLSP)**.

It demonstrates how to translate business drivers into enterprise-ready architecture through requirements analysis, architecture design, technology evaluation, security, reliability, governance, operational considerations, trade-offs, and measurable business value.

The case studies use Google Cloud Platform (GCP) as a technology mapping reference while emphasizing transferable, cloud-neutral architecture principles.

## Architecture Method

Each case study follows a business-led architecture approach:

1. Business capability and business drivers
2. Enterprise requirements and constraints
3. Functional and non-functional requirements
4. Architecture concepts and design considerations
5. Cloud-neutral architecture explanation
6. Cloud service mapping and implementation options
7. Enterprise scenario and architecture flow
8. Trade-offs and Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)
9. Security, reliability, scalability, and operational considerations
10. Cost governance and optimization
11. Best practices and common mistakes
12. Interview questions and architect's notes

## Core Architecture Principles

- Business outcomes drive technology selection.
- Security and governance are designed in from the beginning.
- Availability and recovery are aligned with business criticality.
- Architecture decisions are explicit and supported by trade-off analysis.
- Observability supports measurable reliability and faster diagnosis.
- Cost is evaluated against business value, performance, and resilience.
- Cloud services are selected based on workload fit, constraints, and operational capability.

## Relationship to Project LEDGER

PPD-GVAR focuses on **enterprise architecture reasoning and case-study design**.

Project LEDGER is a separate, self-directed portfolio project for a fictional financial-services organization, FinCore Bank. It demonstrates hands-on GCP infrastructure, security, reliability, observability, and cost-governance work.

Together, the two portfolios demonstrate complementary skills: architecture decision-making and practical implementation.
